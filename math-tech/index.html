How to read this page

Every formula below is copied from the implementation, not from a brochure. Where a component is a deterministic rule or a heuristic rather than a formula, we describe the algorithm exactly as it runs and do not invent mathematics for it.

Lotto Simulator is an analytical, educational and simulation environment. It generates combinations with sixteen methods, reviews them in a Mathematical Court of twelve jurors, six defence counsel and a Supreme Judge, tests real draw histories for fairness, analyses calendar coincidences and replays a physical 3D lottery drum. None of this changes the probability of any number in an official draw — section 8 explains why, with the numbers.

1. System architecture

One application, one code base: the same web app runs in desktop and mobile browsers and, unchanged, inside the iOS and Android apps (Capacitor WebView). Heavy and paid computation runs on the server; everything the user sees is rendered on the device.

  1. Device (web, iOS, Android)Interface, ticket and working sets, the four FREE generator methods, frequency charts, χ² test, calendar maths, 3D drum. Pure random and the 3D drum take their randomness from the Web Crypto API.
  2. Edge functions (TypeScript, Deno)The twelve PRO generator methods, all nineteen Court models, the twelve jurors, six counsel, the Supreme Judge, wheel designs, the prediction ledger. The Court runs on seeded, reproducible random streams.
  3. Database (PostgreSQL)Official draw results with their rule version, payout tables, accounts and entitlements, the append-only prediction ledger and its evaluation.
  4. Data pipelineScheduled jobs collect official results, validate every draw against the rules of its era and publish the current-rules data set.

Who computes what

  • In the browser: the FREE methods (Hot numbers, Combined, Segment coverage, Pure random) and all descriptive statistics. The public build physically removes the PRO implementations from the client: their functions are replaced by stubs, so a PRO result can only come from the server.
  • On the server: every PRO method, the whole Mathematical Court and the Supreme Judge. A request carries the lottery, the method, the number of rows (1–50) and the analysis window; the server loads the draws itself, so the client cannot feed it invented history.
  • Fail-closed rule: a server model needs at least 5 draws in the selected analysis window. With fewer it refuses with an explicit "insufficient history" answer instead of producing rows from nothing.

Reproducible Court computation

Every juror, counsel and model inside the Court runs on its own pseudo-random stream whose seed is a hash of the algorithm version, the persona, the lottery, the complete draw history and the target draw date. The same data therefore always produces the same verdict, and any change to the history changes every stream.

Persistence and provenance

Every combination carries a provenance record: its source (generator method, juror, jury consensus, wheel, 3D draw, Supreme Judge, manual entry), the numbers it started with and an append-only event log of reviews, proposals, counsel positions, verdicts, decisions and transformations (at most 200 events). A record is accepted only if replaying its transformations on the initial numbers reproduces the row exactly; otherwise the row is shown as having an unavailable source.

  • Lineage: when a row is edited by hand, a new record is created whose parent is the previous one (up to four earlier records are kept), so the history before the manual change remains visible.
  • Prediction ledger: every recommendation (a generated row, a juror's replacement, a counsel's position, a verdict) is written to a ledger before the target draw and evaluated automatically after the official result.

2. Main generator: sixteen methods

The generator offers sixteen selections. Four FREE methods run in the browser; twelve PRO methods run on the server. All of them pick kk distinct numbers from 1..N1..N plus the bonus numbers of the lottery; they differ only in how numbers are weighted or constructed.

Shared building blocks

Weighted methods assign a weight wn≥0w_n \ge 0 to every number and then draw sequentially without replacement: each pick removes the number and renormalises the remaining weights.

In plain words Only the first pick follows the weights exactly; later picks are renormalised over the numbers still available. A uniform pick is the special case wn=1w_n = 1.

Randomness: in the browser, Pure random and the uniqueness step use the Web Crypto API, while Hot numbers, Combined and Segment coverage use the browser's standard generator (Math.random). On the server the methods draw from cryptographic randomness, except the Physical model (standard generator) and the Chaos seed, which mixes cryptographic randomness with a high-resolution timer. Bonus numbers

Bonus numbers (Eurojackpot 2 of 12, EuroMillions 2 of 12, Powerball 1 of 26, Mega Ball 1 of 24, Viking number 1 of 5, Powerball AU 1 of 20) are drawn from their own pool, independently of the main numbers. Lotto, SuperEnalotto and Lotto Max have no bonus number for the player: their extra ball is drawn by the operator from the main drum.

FREE filters and uniqueness

Rows produced in the browser pass three filters that are switched on by default: not all numbers even or all odd; the sum inside a range; no run of more than three consecutive numbers. A rejected row is replaced by a uniformly random row that passes the filters (its bonus numbers are drawn with the Bayes weights cj′+2c'_j + 2).

To avoid handing out the same combination twice on the same device, each FREE row is identified by the lexicographic rank of its main numbers among all (Nk)\binom{N}{k} combinations and compared with the last 20 000 rows issued for that lottery; a repeated rank is altered by random swaps, and as a last resort the next unused rank is taken from a random affine permutation of all ranks. A row altered in this way is not checked against the filters again.

Physical model of the drum

PROServer

Purpose: let a simplified 2D simulation decide the order of exit.

NN discs of radius r=clamp⁡(0.12/N, 0.006, 0.018)r = \operatorname{clamp}(0.12/\sqrt N,\ 0.006,\ 0.018) move in a unit square with random air gusts, a constant drift towards the outlet, damping and elastic collisions of equal masses; a disc leaving through the outlet in the middle of the bottom wall is drawn.

Advanced details
Limitations Only up to 18 random pairs are tested for collision per step. This is not the 3D drum of section 4, and it does not use history.

3. The Mathematical Court

The Court reviews a combination you already have. Jurors and models may propose replacing one number; defence counsel argue for or against each proposal; the Supreme Judge rules on a structural field of past draws. Numbers change only when you decide — and every step is recorded.

The order of a case

  • Proposal. A juror or a model scans every possible single swap of the row and proposes at most one replacement — or proposes to keep the row.
  • Defence. Counsel take a position on each proposal: support, objection or protest. A counsel never proposes or applies a number.
  • Verdict. The Supreme Judge accepts the proposal, offers an alternative or keeps the number, using the structural field of past draws.
  • Decision. Only you change numbers: accept a proposal, follow the defence, accept the Judge, keep the original or revert. Each decision is stored with its author.

From a model to a per-number preference

Every model is reduced to a relative preference rel⁡(n)\operatorname{rel}(n) for each number, where 1 means "the model's average", together with a standard error. Models defined by an explicit weight vector are exact; the others are measured by running them:

In plain words The value 1.0 is exactly what a uniform model would give: k/Nk/N is the chance that a given number appears in a uniform row.

The Supreme Judge

The Judge rules on a disputed number using the structural field FF, normalised to its maximum: ϕ(n)=F(n)/max⁡F\phi(n) = F(n)/\max F. A number is weak if it lies in the lowest 35% of the field.

Example Normalised field: current 0.50, threshold 0.55, proposal 0.56, best alternative 0.95. Without protests → accept the proposal. If two of three counsel protest, the bar is 0.580.58 → alternative 0.95. A current number at 0.70 is kept whatever the jury voted.
In plain words Jury votes never move the verdict; they are only reported as agreement or disagreement. The defence can only raise the bar.

Supreme Judge analysis and recommendation

For whole rows the Judge reports a row score and suggests replacing up to ⌊k/2⌋\lfloor k/2 \rfloor of the weakest numbers below θ\theta by the strongest numbers outside the row. You choose which swaps to apply. With no rows, the Judge recommends: uniform random drafts are improved in the same way and returned with their provenance.

Decisions, batches and history

  • Decisions: accept the proposal, accept the defence, accept the Judge, keep the original, revert. Numbers change only when you accept a proposal, accept a Judge verdict that replaces the number, or revert; each change is a transformation in the provenance record.
  • Bulk decisions: at most one replacement per number. "Accept all majority replacements" applies only options chosen by more than half of the selected jurors; "Apply all proposals" takes, for each number, the majority option or else the one with the most votes, and skips ties.
  • Batches: a review of up to 50 rows by the same line-up of personas counts as one attempt; rows already analysed are skipped and an interrupted batch resumes with the same plan.
  • History: event types are jury review, model review, proposal, defence review, Judge review, decision and transformation; the log keeps up to 200 events and never drops a transformation or the latest reviews.

4. The 3D drum: physics engine

The 3D draw is a real-time rigid-body simulation: balls are bodies with mass, inertia, friction and restitution, pushed by gravity, a stirring rotor and an air field, and the drawn ball is the one that physically falls through the outlet. It is a credible physical model of a drum, not a recording of a real machine.

Engine and coordinates

Physics runs in Rapier 3D (version 0.14, WebAssembly); rendering uses Three.js (r169). The world is right-handed with +y+y up and the origin at the centre of the chamber; the rotor axis is the horizontal xx axis and +z+z points towards the viewer.

  • Chamber: a sphere of radius R=3.0R = 3.0 units, built as a triangle mesh with inward normals and a circular outlet at the bottom (half-angle arcsin⁡(0.34/3)≈6.5∘\arcsin(0.34/3) \approx 6.5^\circ).
  • Balls: spheres of radius r=0.2r = 0.2 and density 1; one ball per number, from 5 to 90 balls.
  • Rotor: a kinematic shaft with six arms whose capsule paddles are sized to stop 0.14 units from the glass in their plane of rotation, plus a smaller three-arm rotor whose direction alternates between mix phases.
  • Gate: a cylindrical plug in the outlet, removed when a ball may leave.

Scale and Froude similarity

One model unit is 10 cm: a 40 mm ball is 0.4 units and the chamber is a 600 mm sphere. Gravity is reduced to 3030 units/s² instead of the real 98.198.1. Motion dominated by gravity and inertia looks the same when the Froude number is the same, so the simulation corresponds to a real-size machine with a slower clock:

In plain words The design rule stated in the configuration is that drag, suction and every rate must be expressed in this same scaled base. A physics regression test checks the length unit and the ball mass; the other rates follow the configuration.

Forces

Every ball obeys Newton's second law with the forces that the code actually applies:

Air field and quadratic drag

The blower is modelled as a prescribed air-velocity field u(x,t)\mathbf u(\mathbf x, t); each ball feels quadratic drag towards the local air velocity. The drag constant is fixed by a terminal speed of 48 units/s:

  • Swirl: a horizontal rotation of 11 units/s about the vertical axis.
  • Inward flow: beyond 60% of the radius, up to 50 (∣x∣−1.8)/1.250\,(|\mathbf x| - 1.8)/1.2 towards the centre, so balls keep cycling instead of hugging the glass.
  • Turbulence: smooth three-dimensional value noise of amplitude 58 that drifts in time; it is a fixed deterministic function, not a random number generator.
  • Anti-stall gusts: a ball that stays too long at the bottom, at the top, at the wall or almost motionless gets a local gust added to the air velocity, still acting through drag.

Analytic spherical wall

On top of the mesh, the glass is enforced as an exact sphere at the velocity level, which prevents tunnelling and gives correct rolling. With outward normal n^\hat{\mathbf n}, outward speed vnv_n and penetration ξ=∣x∣−(R−r−0.06)\xi = |\mathbf x| - (R - r - 0.06):

Time stepping and integration

The simulation advances in fixed steps independent of the screen refresh rate; each frame runs as many fixed steps as real time requires, at most five:

How a ball is selected

After 1 s of start-up and 2.6 s of mixing the gate opens. While the outlet is open the updraft above it collapses smoothly, the central jet is reduced to 62% and a suction of about 2.2 ball weights (about 4 after 5 s) pulls balls near the outlet:

The drawn ball is the first one whose centre falls below y=−3.18y = -3.18; if several do in the same step, the lowest. No random number is consulted at that moment. The random numbers of a draw are the initial placement of the balls and a small initial velocity and spin, taken from the Web Crypto API (again when a separate bonus pool is loaded). The ball then travels along the exit path to the rack; that motion is scripted, not simulated.

Rendering versus simulation versus randomness

  • Simulation: fixed 1/60 s steps; quality presets (ultra, high, medium, low) change shadows, bloom, resolution and mesh detail only, never the physics.
  • Rendering: each frame shows the last completed step; there is no interpolation between steps.
  • Randomness: the forces are deterministic; the outcome depends on the initial placement and, from the second ball on, also on how long the reveal of the previous ball takes on the device, so a draw cannot be replayed exactly.
Limitations Not simulated: the chamber does not rotate; the air is a prescribed field, not computational fluid dynamics (no pressure, no wakes, no Magnus effect); every ball has identical mass and size; the exit tube and rack have no colliders. The simulated drum does not reproduce the trajectories or results of any real lottery machine, and official draws are independent of it.

6. Calendar analysis

Calendar analysis looks for coincidences between draw dates and drawn numbers — for example the day of the month appearing among the balls — and compares each observed rate with the rate that pure chance would produce.

Selecting draws

Draws can be filtered by year, month, the same month across all years or a date range, and by rule era. Frequency lists (hot, cold, longest absent) use raw counts in the selection; a number that is impossible in every selected era is left out, and a selection that mixes eras is flagged with a warning, because a number valid only in the newer era looks falsely "cold".
